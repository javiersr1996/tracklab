# Football bundle format (SkillCorner opendata)

Generated by `tracklab freeze-schema football`. Every figure below is measured on the 20 matches of the frozen bundle; the per-match rows live in `pipeline/reports/football_format.csv`, and the tables here are rendered from them.

## Read this first

Four things in this bundle mislead without ever raising an error:

1. **Events and phases are not in the tracking coordinate frame.** They are normalised so the attacker always goes towards +x; map (x, y) to (-x, -y) when `attacking_side` is `right_to_left`, or half of every match comes out mirrored. [Measured](#the-events-and-phases-are-not-in-the-tracking-coordinate-frame).
2. **`end_frame` declares a range, not the frames that exist.** The declared last frame is missing in 6 of 20 files; never join tracking against a generated range. [Measured](#end-frame-declares-a-range-not-existing-frames).
3. **`events.player_id` is not a foreign key.** One id with 35 event rows exists neither in `players[]` nor in the tracking. [Measured](#events-player_id-is-not-an-enforceable-foreign-key).
4. **Two tracking layouts.** 5 matches (2011166, 2013725, 2015213, 2016236, 2017461) carry pre-match, half-time and post-match footage as null frames. [Measured](#two-tracking-layouts).

## The four traps, measured

### The events and phases are NOT in the tracking coordinate frame

**`dynamic_events.csv` and `phases_of_play.csv` are attack-normalised: the team in possession always attacks towards +x, and +y is the attacker's left. The tracking JSONL is in the fixed pitch frame. Joining them without rotating by `attacking_side` mirrors half of every match, and nothing errors.** Measured by comparing each event's (`x_start`, `y_start`) with the tracking position of the same `player_id` at `frame_start`, 2 m tolerance, 3922 to 5655 events per match: with `attacking_side = left_to_right` the raw coordinates match in 96.39 to 100% of events; with `right_to_left` the rotated (-x, -y) match in 95.01 to 100%; the two wrong combinations match in 0 to 0.28% and 0.04 to 0.36%. Events in a `*_left` channel have y > 0 in 100% (ltr) and 100% (rtl) of cases. **To map an event or phase into the tracking frame: keep (x, y) when `attacking_side` is `left_to_right`, use (-x, -y) when it is `right_to_left`.**

### Two hypotheses the plan carried, refuted by the data

- *The tracking indexes players by `trackable_object`.* **Refuted in every match.** `player_data[].player_id` is `players[].id`: of the 29 to 32 distinct ids per match, 29 to 32 are found in `players[].id`, 0 in `trackable_object` and 0 in `team_player_id`. `possession.player_id` and the events' `player_id` use `players[].id` too. `ball_data` carries no id at all (keys `is_detected,x,y,z`), so `ball.trackable_object` = 55 is not a join key either.
- *Football tracking is intermittent, unlike basketball's complete-or-empty frames.* **Refuted at frame level in every match.** In-period frames carry 22 players or none: partial frames (1-21) are 0% and the ball is present exactly when the 22 are. The intermittence lives per player in `is_detected` (49.1 to 65.8% of entries detected; frames with all 22 detected: 0%). Bronze needs no partial-frame logic; silver must keep `is_detected` per object.

### Events player_id is not an enforceable foreign key

**`events.player_id` is not a foreign key bronze can enforce.** match 1953632: `player_id` 973077 has 35 event rows and exists neither in `players[]` nor in the tracking (19 other matches are clean). Load events as they come and report the orphan rows; do not drop them and do not declare the relationship.

### Two tracking layouts

15 matches start period 1 at frame 0-10 with 9 to 29 frames outside the periods. 5 matches (2011166, 2013725, 2015213, 2016236, 2017461) keep pre-match, half-time and post-match footage: period 1 starts at frame 1170-4040, 11059 to 13459 frames lie outside the periods (`timestamp` and `period` null, no players, no ball) and the file runs 390 to 1030 frames past `end_frame`.

### End frame declares a range, not existing frames

**`end_frame` declares a range; it does not guarantee that every frame of the range exists.** `frame_max - end_frame` of the last period is 0 in 7 matches, -1 in 6 (the declared last frame of period 2 is missing from the file), +10 in 2 and +390 to +1030 in the 5 with post-match footage (per match under Frames and clock). Any join between tracking and periods must go against the frames that exist, never against a range built with `range()` or `generate_series`: that would invent frames the provider never delivered, which is fabricating data.

### What the contract test can assert

- `frame` contiguous from 0 in every file: a hard invariant, measured in all 20 matches.
- `frame_max - end_frame[last period]` reported as a number per match, never as a boolean: `frame_max == end_frame` fails in 13 of 20 matches, not because the data is wrong but because equality is the wrong rule, and a boolean would also hide that there are three regimes.
- In-period frames carry 22 players or 0, and a ball exactly when 22.
- The 322 and 44 column headers are byte-identical to the frozen ones (`header_sha256` in the schema files).

## Tracking JSONL

One JSON object per line with keys `ball_data,frame,image_corners_projection,period,player_data,possession,timestamp`. `player_data` is a list of `{is_detected,player_id,x,y}`, `ball_data` is `{is_detected,x,y,z}`, `possession` is `{group,player_id}` and `image_corners_projection` has `x_bottom_left,x_bottom_right,x_top_left,x_top_right,y_bottom_left,y_bottom_right,y_top_left,y_top_right`.

### Frames and clock

- `frame` is the line index: contiguous from 0 in every match (yes). The file does not always end at the `end_frame` of the last period: `frame_max - end_frame` per match is 1874553: 0, 1886347: 0, 1899585: -1, 1925299: 0, 1927964: -1, 1953632: -1, 1959846: 0, 1986691: -1, 1996435: 0, 1996436: -1, 2006229: -1, 2006363: 10, 2007448: 0, 2007721: 10, 2010085: 0, 2011166: 460, 2013725: 390, 2015213: 750, 2016236: 550, 2017461: 1030 (negative: the declared last frame is missing; positive: post-match footage).
- `match.json.match_periods[]` gives `start_frame` and `end_frame` per period. Frames outside every period (before kick-off and between halves) carry `timestamp: null`, `period: null`, an empty `player_data` and a null ball: null timestamp, null period and outside-period coincide exactly in every match (yes). Outside-period frames per match: 9 to 13459: 15 matches start period 1 at frame 0-10 and cut the half-time out, the other 5 keep pre-match and half-time footage in the file (period 1 starts at frame 1170-4040 and period 2 some 9000-10000 frames after the end of period 1).
- `timestamp` is the string `HH:MM:SS.ff` (format matched in 100% of non-null timestamps) and is a **match clock, not a period clock**: `timestamp = 45:00 x (period - 1) + (frame - start_frame[period]) / 10`. Verified for 100% of non-null frames; the first frame of period 2 reads `00:45:00.00`.
- The events CSV uses a different string for the same clock, `MM:SS.f` with one decimal and minutes beyond 59 (`45:02.3`). `time_start` equals the clock of `frame_start` in 92.24 to 94.32% of events; when it does not, it equals the clock of `frame_physical_start` (5.68 to 7.76% of events, all of type on_ball_engagement), leaving 0 unexplained per match.

### Coverage

- Inside the periods a frame carries **either 22 players or none**: 22 players in 65.8 to 81.04% of in-period frames, 0 in 18.96 to 34.2%, partial frames (1-21) in 0%, frames with more than 22: 0. The ball is present exactly when the 22 are: yes in every match.
- The intermittence SkillCorner documents lives in `is_detected`, not in presence: only 49.1 to 65.8% of player entries are detected, and a frame with all 22 detected occurs in 0% of in-period frames. The rest are extrapolated positions (`_extrapolated` in the file name). `ball_data.is_detected` is true in 75.45 to 85.38% of frames with a ball. The frame-level rule (complete or empty) is the same one measured in the basketball bundle, so the conformed schema needs no partial-frame logic, but it must keep `is_detected` per object because in football most positions are extrapolated.
- `ball_data.z` is non-null exactly when the ball is present (65.8 to 81.04% of in-period frames), in metres: min -0.66 to -0.13, median 0.19 to 0.28, max 12.11 to 14.7, negative in 0.61 to 4.79% of the values.

### Coordinates and orientation

- Pitch `pitch_length` 105 x `pitch_width` 68, origin at the centre spot, x along the length. Observed per-match player extremes: x_min -60.12 to -54.63, x_max 55.5 to 63.51, y_min -45.67 to -37.14, y_max 37.05 to 47.36 (players step outside the lines).
- `home_team_side` is a list with one entry per period, e.g. `['left_to_right', 'right_to_left']`. Read as: `left_to_right` means the home team attacks towards +x in that period. Checked against the mean x of each team's goalkeeper per period (`gk_home_mean_x`, `gk_away_mean_x` in the table): `ok` in every match.
- **The events and phases CSVs are not in the tracking frame.** Comparing each event's (`x_start`, `y_start`) with the tracking position of `player_id` at `frame_start` (2 m tolerance): `attack_normalized` in every match. With the attacking side `left_to_right` the raw coordinates match (96.39 to 100%); with `right_to_left` only the rotated (-x, -y) match (95.01 to 100%). So in events and phases the team in possession always attacks towards +x, and `attacking_side` tells which rotation maps them back to the tracking frame.
- Sign of y in that attack-normalised frame: events in a `*_left` channel have y > 0 in 100% (ltr) and 100% (rtl) of cases, so **+y is the attacker's left**. Phase `third_start` labels agree with the same frame in 99.1 to 100% of phases.

### Identifiers

- `match.json.players[]` carries three ids: `id`, `team_player_id` and `trackable_object`; the ball is `ball.trackable_object` = 55.
- The tracking `player_data[].player_id` is **`players[].id`**, not `trackable_object`: `id` in every match (29 to 32 distinct ids per match, 29 to 32 found in `players[].id`, 0 in `trackable_object`). `possession.player_id` uses the same key in every match, and so does the events' `player_id` in 19 matches; not in 1953632. Events can name a player absent from `players[]` and from the tracking: 0 to 1 such ids per match, 0 to 35 event rows (see `ev_player_ids_not_in_players` per match), so `events.player_id -> match_players` is not a foreign key bronze can enforce.

### Frame-level possession

- `possession.group` (`home team` / `away team` / null) is set in 45.76 to 63.33% of in-period frames and `possession.player_id` in 12.93 to 24.89%. Of the frames with a group, 94.43 to 97.49% fall inside a phase and the group agrees with the phase's `team_in_possession_id` in 76.65 to 87.61% of those.

## match.json

Keys: `id`, scores, `date_time`, `stadium`, `home_team`/`away_team` (`id`, `name`, `short_name`, `acronym`), kits (`jersey_color`, `number_color`), coaches, `home_team_playing_time`/`away_team_playing_time`, `competition_edition`, `match_periods[]` (`period`, `name`, `start_frame`, `end_frame`, `duration_frames`, `duration_minutes`), `competition_round`, `referees`, `players[]`, `status`, `home_team_side[]`, `ball`, `pitch_length`, `pitch_width`. Each player has `player_role` (`id`, `position_group`, `name`, `acronym`), `start_time`/`end_time`, `number`, cards, `playing_time` (`total` and `by_period`, with frames), `team_player_id`, `team_id`, `id`, names, `birthday`, `trackable_object`, `gender`. 35 to 36 players per match are listed, 29 to 32 appear in the tracking.

## dynamic_events.csv

- 322 columns, identical header in the 20 files (sha256 `801a67ffefdb68b215b1205318267fae6c3bd14316e1d305ef7775f9b2a74f48`). Rows per match: 3923 to 5656; `index` is contiguous from 0: yes in every match.
- `event_type` takes 4 values: off_ball_run, on_ball_engagement, passing_option, player_possession (per-match counts in the CSV column `event_types`). **There is no shot event**: a shot is a `player_possession` whose `end_type` is `shot` (15 to 34 per match). `end_type` of player possessions: `pass=630;possession_loss=71;shot=32;clearance=16;foul_suffered=7;unknown=1` in the first match, same vocabulary elsewhere. `lead_to_shot` is a wider flag (33 to 107 player possessions per match lead to a shot).
- Columns that are empty in all 20 files, typed VARCHAR by default: none.

## phases_of_play.csv

- 44 columns, identical header in the 20 files (sha256 `1dca435ea3cac484f53916f23db1c99da23ae371dd1d637c1cb95b421a80cab3`). Rows per match: 383 to 490.
- **Phase, team possession and player possession are three different units.** A *player possession* is a `player_possession` event (757 to 1140 per match). A *phase* is a row here: one team, one `team_in_possession_phase_type` (build_up, create, finish, direct, chaotic, set_play, quick_break, transition), contiguous frames. A *team possession* is not a table: it is the run of consecutive same-team phases, and the events mark its ends with `first_player_possession_in_team_possession` / `last_...` (167 to 263 per match, against 169 to 249 same-team runs of phases; the difference is the 45 to 92 phases per match that have no exported player possession). 36.5 to 48.62% of team possessions span more than one phase.
- `team_possession_loss_in_phase` is true exactly when the next phase of the period belongs to the other team: mismatches per match 1 to 6. A phase can therefore hold the end of a team possession, which is why phase and possession are not the same unit.
- Consecutive phases overlap 0 times and leave a gap (dead ball) in 17.92 to 32.83% of pairs; phases cover 45.08 to 63.06% of in-period frames.
- `n_player_possessions_in_phase` equals the number of exported `player_possession` rows of the phase in only 60.57 to 66.19% of phases: the counter comes from the provider's full possession list, the CSV exports a subset.
